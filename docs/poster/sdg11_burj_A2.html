1The ground slows the wind.

u(z) ∝ ln((z + z₀) ÷ z₀)
a logarithm: fast high up, slow near the ground.

2So the push grows with height.

The front is pushed in, harder higher up, growing like (u ÷ U)² (dashed). The sides and back are sucked outward.

3F ∝ v²: double the wind, four times the push.

A category 5 hurricane pushes with 79.2 MN, about the weight of 8,000 tonnes. Turning effect at the base = force × distance: 23.7 GN·m ÷ 79.2 MN = 299 m up the tower.

How a building is different.

  • It stands on the ground. A plane flies in even air; a tower meets wind that changes with height.
  • It is blunt. Air breaks away at its edges, leaving a big wake. It needs no lift, only to resist the push.
